Sexual-Harassment Cases at Workplace Still Under Adjudication, 50 Lags

By Tanisha Jaiswal

Workplace sexual harassment complaints had the lowest resolution rate among categories recorded by the Maharashtra State Commission for Women in 2022-23 with only 20 of 70 cases marked resolved. The remaining 50 cases were still under proceedings, this gave the category a resolution rate of 28.6%.

That was less than half the commission’s overall resolution rate of 63.5% for the year. The commission received 16,012 complaints during April 2022-March 2023, out of which 10,172 were disposed of and 5,840 were under proceedings. Sexual harassment complaints in the workplace accounted for a small portion of the overall caseload but had a considerably lower recorded resolution rate compared with other categories.

There is a lot of variation in the data by complaint type. Complaints regarding property had the highest resolution rate at 72.1%. “Others” came in at second with 70.1% and complaints for social problems, rape and related complaints at 67.7%. The rate of resolution of complaints relating to marital problems was 54.7 per cent and of complaints of harassment at the workplace was 55.3 per cent. The only issue with less than 30% resolution in the dataset was workplace sexual-harassment.

But when you begin to look at the number of cases with the rates, that difference begins to get really big. Of these, the commission received 70 complaints on workplace sexual-harassment, 5,193 on marital problems and 4,462 on social problems, rape and related issues. Of those 70 workplace sexual-harassment cases, 50 were ongoing. This means that the 28.6% rate is 20 resolved cases out of a relatively small pool, not thousands of complaints.

There remained other categories of the commission’s wider work to be done. Of the 5,840 cases pending, 2,352 or roughly 40.3 per cent, were on account of matrimonial problems, the largest part of the pending workload. Social problems, rape and related complaints were other categories, at 24.7%. “Others” made up 21%. These three combined represented approximately 86% of cases in process.

About the data. Figures come from complaint tables published by the Maharashtra State Commission for Women, scraped and analysed in Python. Resolution rate = resolved complaints ÷ total complaints (old pending plus newly registered) in each 12-month period, April to March.
